Recommendations

Small things that can help when life feels heavy. Try one. You don't need to do them all. Under school pressure? Jump to study apps and tips.

For right now

Slow breathing

Breathe in for four counts, then out for six. Repeat for two minutes. Longer exhales help the body settle.

5-4-3-2-1 grounding

Name five things you see, four you feel, three you hear, two you smell, and one you taste.

Listen to something calm

Put on soft sounds and rest your eyes. Try our calming sounds.

For your day

Write it out

Spend five minutes writing what's on your mind. Use the reflection space to start.

Move a little

A ten-minute walk or some stretching can ease tension. Small counts.

Protect your sleep

Keep a steady bedtime, and put your phone away for the last half hour of the day.

Take breaks from the news

Choose set times to check news and social media, and let the rest of the day be quiet.

Eat and drink water

Low food and water can make stress feel louder. Look after the basics first.

Do one small thing

Make your bed, reply to one message, or tidy one corner. Gentle progress counts.

Study tips

Study in short blocks

Work for 25 minutes, rest for 5, and repeat. After four rounds, take a longer break.

Test yourself

Close the book and try to recall what you read. It works better than rereading.

Space it out

Review a little every day instead of cramming the night before.

Break big tasks down

Turn "revise chemistry" into "do ten questions on one topic." Small goals feel doable.

Sleep before exams

Rest helps memory. A good night's sleep usually beats a late-night cram.

Ask for help early

Talk to a teacher, lecturer, or classmate when you're stuck. It's a smart move, not a weakness.

Steady your nerves

Before a test, breathe out slowly a few times and remind yourself you've prepared as best you can.

Stop comparing

Other people's results don't measure your worth. Focus on your own progress.

Plan breaks and fun

Rest, food, movement, and time with friends are part of studying well.

Reach out

Talk to someone you trust

A friend, family member, or faith leader. You don't need the right words. "I've been struggling" is enough.

Speak to a professional

If low mood, anxiety, or pressure lasts for more than a couple of weeks or affects daily life, a doctor or licensed counsellor can help.

If you feel unsafe or at risk of harming yourself, contact your local emergency number or a crisis line near you right now.
